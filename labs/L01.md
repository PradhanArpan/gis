## 🧠 Background Theory: What is Georeferencing?

> **Georeferencing** is the process of establishing the relationship between image pixel coordinates $(u, v)$ on a scanned paper map or aerial photograph and real-world earth surface coordinates $(X, Y)$ within a defined Geodetic Datum and Coordinate Reference System (CRS).

```
PIXEL SPACE (Scanned Map)                 REAL-WORLD GEODETIC SPACE
+-----------------------+                 (Latitude / Longitude on WGS 84)
| (0,0)           (u,0) |                       27° 30' N
|   +---------------+   |                 88° 15' E +-----------+ 88° 30' E
|   | Neatline Grid |   |    Polynomial             |           |
|   | Intersections |   |   Transformation          | Toposheet |
|   +---------------+   |  =================>       | Quad      |
| (0,v)           (u,v) |                           | 15' x 15' |
+-----------------------+                 88° 15' E +-----------+ 88° 30' E
  Measured in Pixels                                    27° 15' N
```

### Survey of India Open Series Maps (OSM) System:
- **Scale:** $1:50,000$ (covers a geographic extent of $15' \text{ latitude} \times 15' \text{ longitude}$).
- **Sheet Numbering:**
  - Modern OSM Index: **G45E7**
  - Older National Grid Index: **78 A/7**
  - Location: Northern West Bengal / Sikkim sub-Himalayan region.
- **Geographic Bounding Coordinates for G45E7 / 78A/7**:
  - **West Longitude ($X_{\min}$)**: $88^\circ 15' 00''\text{ E} = 88.25^\circ$
  - **East Longitude ($X_{\max}$)**: $88^\circ 30' 00''\text{ E} = 88.50^\circ$
  - **South Latitude ($Y_{\min}$)**: $27^\circ 15' 00''\text{ N} = 27.25^\circ$
  - **North Latitude ($Y_{\max}$)**: $27^\circ 30' 00''\text{ N} = 27.50^\circ$
- **Graticule Spacing:** Intermediate grid tick marks are spaced every $5'$ ($88^\circ 20'$, $88^\circ 25'$ Longitude and $27^\circ 20'$, $27^\circ 25'$ Latitude).

## 🛠️ Step-by-Step Hands-On Procedure

### Phase 1: Downloading the Toposheet from Survey of India Portal

:::steps
1. Open the [Survey of India Online Maps Portal](https://onlinemaps.surveyofindia.gov.in/FreeMapSpecification.aspx).
2. Log in with your registered mobile number / email credentials.
3. In the **Open Series Map (Free PDF)** search bar, enter `G45E7` or `78A/7` (or select State: *West Bengal*, District: *Darjeeling / Kalimpong*).
4. Click **Search Sheet Number** $\rightarrow$ select the sheet from the table $\rightarrow$ click **Download**.
5. Save the downloaded PDF to your local downloads directory as `G45E7_78A7.pdf`.
:::

---

### Phase 2: Launching QGIS & Opening Georeferencer

:::steps
1. Start **QGIS Desktop 4.0 / 3.x**.
2. From the top menu bar, click **Layer $\rightarrow$ Georeferencer...**  
   *(In older QGIS versions, it is located under `Raster → Georeferencer`).*
3. The Georeferencer workbench opens in a separate window.
4. Click **Open Raster** (`Ctrl + O` or the first icon on the top toolbar).
5. In the file selection dialog, set the file type filter to **All Files (*)** or **PDF (*.pdf)**.
6. Browse to `C:\Users\HP\Downloads\G45E7_78A7.pdf` and click **Open**.
7. If prompted for raster rendering DPI, accept the default **300 DPI**. The full topographical mapsheet will render on the canvas.
:::

---

### Phase 3: Setting the Transformation Parameters

Before placing ground control points, configure the mathematical rectification model:

:::steps
1. Click the yellow gear icon (**Transformation Settings**) on the toolbar.
2. In the dialog, set:
   - **Transformation Type**: `Polynomial 1st order` (affine transformation accounting for translation, scale, and rotation). Use `Linear` if scanning distortion is negligible.
   - **Resampling Method**: `Nearest Neighbour` (retains exact raster cell values and sharp grid lines).
   - **Target CRS**: Click the small globe icon, type `4326`, and select **EPSG:4326 - WGS 84** (Geographic 2D Coordinates).
   - **Output Raster**: Browse and name your file: `G45E7_78A7_georeferenced.tif`.
   - **Compression**: Select `LZW` or `DEFLATE` (drastically reduces file size from ~300 MB to ~35 MB).
   - Check $\checkmark$ **Save GCP points** (creates a permanent `.points` table file).
   - Check $\checkmark$ **Generate PDF map** and **Generate PDF report** (specifies paths for submission).
   - Check $\checkmark$ **Load in QGIS when done**.
3. Click **OK** to save the transformation parameters.
:::

---

### Phase 4: Adding Ground Control Points (GCPs)

Ground control points must be well-distributed across the mapsheet—never clustered in one corner. We will pick all 4 outer neatline corners and at least 2 interior graticule intersections.

> [!IMPORTANT] Syntax for Coordinate Entry
> When entering coordinates in the QGIS pop-up window:
> - **X / East (Longitude)**: Enter degrees and minutes separated by a space (e.g. `88 15` for $88^\circ 15'$).
> - **Y / North (Latitude)**: Enter degrees and minutes separated by a space (e.g. `27 30` for $27^\circ 30'$).
> - Do not confuse X and Y! Longitude is always East-West (X), and Latitude is always North-South (Y).

:::steps
1. Select the **Add Point** tool (`Ctrl + A` or the icon with a crosshair and yellow star).
2. **GCP 1 — Top-Left Corner (North-West)**:
   - Zoom in closely to the top-left neatline intersection.
   - Click exactly on the crosshair where the outer border lines intersect.
   - In the pop-up box, enter:  
     **X / East**: `88 15`  
     **Y / North**: `27 30`  
   - Click **OK**.
3. **GCP 2 — Top-Right Corner (North-East)**:
   - Pan to the top-right corner.
   - Click on the corner intersection.
   - Enter:  
     **X / East**: `88 30`  
     **Y / North**: `27 30`  
   - Click **OK**.
4. **GCP 3 — Bottom-Left Corner (South-West)**:
   - Pan to the bottom-left corner.
   - Click on the corner intersection.
   - Enter:  
     **X / East**: `88 15`  
     **Y / North**: `27 15`  
   - Click **OK**.
5. **GCP 4 — Bottom-Right Corner (South-East)**:
   - Pan to the bottom-right corner.
   - Click on the corner intersection.
   - Enter:  
     **X / East**: `88 30`  
     **Y / North**: `27 15`  
   - Click **OK**.
6. **GCP 5 & 6 — Interior 5' Graticule Ticks**:
   - Pan to the interior crosshairs marked by `+` ticks inside the map body:
   - Point 5: Intersection of $88^\circ 20'\text{ E}$ and $27^\circ 20'\text{ N}$ $\rightarrow$ Enter `88 20` and `27 20`.
   - Point 6: Intersection of $88^\circ 25'\text{ E}$ and $27^\circ 25'\text{ N}$ $\rightarrow$ Enter `88 25` and `27 25`.
:::

> [!CAUTION] Error Threshold Rule
> The **Total Mean RMS Error** shown in the bottom status bar must be strictly **less than 1.0 pixel** (ideally $\le 0.3 - 0.5$ pixels). If any single GCP shows a large red residual vector or error $> 2$ pixels:
> 1. Check whether X and Y coordinates were accidentally swapped.
> 2. Use the **Move GCP Point** tool to re-center the marker on the true line intersection.
> 3. Or use **Delete Point** (`Ctrl + D`) and re-pick the point carefully.

## ❓ Viva & Diagnostic Questions

1. **Why is WGS 84 (EPSG:4326) used instead of Everest 1830 datum for modern Open Series Maps?**  
   *Answer:* Older Survey of India sheets used the Everest 1830 ellipsoid with Indian Datum. Modern Open Series Maps (OSM) published after 2005 are compliant with global GPS and satellite datums (WGS 84 ellipsoid) to facilitate direct integration with satellite imagery.
2. **What is the difference between Linear and 1st Order Polynomial transformation?**  
   *Answer:* Linear transformation performs uniform scaling, translation, and rotation (preserving parallel lines). 1st order polynomial (affine) allows independent scaling along the X and Y axes and handles paper shrinkage or non-orthogonal scanning skew.
3. **What does an RMS error of 0.45 pixels mean in ground distance at 1:50,000 scale?**  
   *Answer:* At 300 DPI (0.084 mm/pixel), 1 pixel represents $0.084 \times 50 = 4.2\text{ m}$ on the ground. An RMS error of 0.45 pixels corresponds to an exceptional sub-2 meter ground accuracy ($\approx 1.89\text{ m}$).
