# Lab 5: Coordinate Reference Systems (CRS) & Projection Transformation

**Course Code:** CE452 | **Credits:** 2 | **Week:** 5  
**Estimated Time:** 120 Minutes | **Mapped Outcome:** CO1 (L3 - Apply)  
**Primary Tools:** QGIS On-the-fly CRS, Reproject Layer, UTM Projections

---

## 🎯 Aim & Objectives
1. Understand the fundamental mathematical distinction between **Geographic Coordinate Systems (GCS)** (degrees on an ellipsoid) and **Projected Coordinate Systems (PCS)** (meters on a flat plane).
2. Differentiate between global ellipsoids (WGS 84) and local historical datums (Everest 1830).
3. Determine the appropriate **Universal Transverse Mercator (UTM)** zone for study areas in India (Zone 43N, 44N, 45N).
4. Perform on-the-fly CRS transformation and permanent vector/raster layer re-projection.

---

## 🛠️ Step-by-Step Hands-On Procedure

### Phase 1: Identifying UTM Zones across India
- India spans longitudes $68^\circ\text{ E}$ to $97^\circ\text{ E}$, falling across UTM Zones **42N, 43N, 44N, 45N, and 46N**:
  - Western India (Gujarat, Maharashtra, Karnataka): **UTM Zone 43N (EPSG:32643)**
  - Central / Southern India (Telangana, Andhra, Tamil Nadu, Odisha): **UTM Zone 44N (EPSG:32644)**
  - Eastern / North-Eastern India (West Bengal, Bihar, Assam, Sikkim): **UTM Zone 45N (EPSG:32645)**

### Phase 2: Permanent Layer Reprojection
:::steps
1. Load your georeferenced toposheet `G45E7_78A7` (which is in `EPSG:4326 - WGS 84` in decimal degrees).
2. Attempting to run distance buffering in degrees ($0.001^\circ$) leads to severe metric distortion.
3. Open the **Processing Toolbox** (`Ctrl + Alt + T`) and search for **Warp (reproject)**.
4. Parameters:
   - Input layer: `G45E7_78A7_georeferenced.tif`
   - Source CRS: `EPSG:4326`
   - Target CRS: Select `EPSG:32645 - WGS 84 / UTM zone 45N`
   - Resampling method: `Bilinear`
   - Output file: `G45E7_78A7_UTM45N.tif`
5. Click **Run**. Inspect the layer properties: coordinates are now expressed in **metric Eastings and Northings**.
:::
